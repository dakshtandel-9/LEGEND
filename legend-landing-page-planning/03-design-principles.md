# 03 — Design Principles

## 1. Editorial before corporate
The page must feel curated like a magazine, not assembled from a SaaS landing-page template.

Use:
- strong typographic hierarchy;
- art-directed image crops;
- asymmetric layouts;
- editorial rules/dividers;
- pull quotes;
- issue labels.

Avoid:
- endless identical rounded cards;
- dashboard-style blocks;
- generic startup gradients;
- excessive glassmorphism.

## 2. People are the visual heroes
LEGEND's strongest asset is the caliber and variety of people/stories in the publication.

Portraits should carry the page.

## 3. Prestige through restraint
Luxury comes from:
- proportion;
- typography;
- whitespace;
- image quality;
- consistent rhythm.

It does not come from adding more effects.

## 4. Translate print DNA; do not imitate a PDF
Use the magazine's:
- masthead feeling;
- serif headlines;
- issue colors;
- pull quotes;
- editorial grids.

But create digital behavior:
- responsive stacks;
- subtle hover states;
- scroll reveals;
- tap-friendly mobile sections.

## 5. Clear hierarchy
A user should understand each item in this order:
`Category → Person / Subject → Story → Issue`

## 6. Master brand stays stable
The whole website should not change identity with every new issue.

Master colors:
- ink;
- paper;
- warm gold.

Edition colors:
- May lime/yellow family;
- July cyan family;
- future issue accents.

## 7. No Mumbai cliché overload
Do not fill the site with Gateway of India, Marine Drive and skyline stock shots unless editorially relevant.

Let Mumbai appear through:
- stories;
- people;
- typography;
- location context.

## 8. Storytelling before conversion
The visitor should first feel:
“This is a serious, premium publication.”

Then:
“I want to read / appear / partner.”

## 9. Mobile is a first-class design
The page must retain prestige on a phone:
- bold crops;
- readable display type;
- clear CTAs;
- no tiny editorial text;
- no desktop-only composition hacks.

## 10. Every visual choice should have a reason
If animation, color or layout does not improve:
- hierarchy;
- mood;
- comprehension;
- credibility;
remove it.
