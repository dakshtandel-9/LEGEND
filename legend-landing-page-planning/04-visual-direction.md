# 04 — Visual Direction

## Direction name
**Contemporary Indian Editorial Luxury**

## Overall mood
Imagine a high-end print magazine translated into a modern website:
- warm paper;
- black editorial ink;
- gold brand detail;
- large portraits;
- oversized serif headlines;
- precise sans-serif labels;
- bold edition colors used sparingly;
- thin graphic rules;
- quiet transitions.

## Visual language

### Backgrounds
Primary:
- warm ivory / paper;
- pure white;
- near-black for occasional contrast sections.

### Headline treatment
- serif;
- large;
- tight line-height;
- not overly centered;
- allow line breaks that feel art-directed.

### Labels
Uppercase sans-serif labels:
- ISSUE 02
- DEVELOPERS' DIARY
- LEADERSHIP
- JULY 2026

### Photography
Use portraits and editorial images at meaningful scale.
Avoid thumbnails everywhere.

### Shapes
Prefer:
- rectangles;
- lines;
- clean image frames.

Use rounded corners only where needed. Do not make all media cards pill-shaped.

## Hero direction
Recommended structure:
- left: masthead copy;
- right/center: editorial portrait or feature composition;
- overlapping issue covers as supporting proof;
- thin line metadata beneath.

## Section transitions
Alternate:
1. ivory;
2. white;
3. image-led;
4. dark editorial strip;
5. ivory.

This gives rhythm without looking like a collection of disconnected blocks.

## Issue accents
May and July show different editorial accent families. Preserve that behavior inside issue components rather than making the entire website follow the latest issue color.

## Texture
Optional:
- extremely subtle paper grain background image;
- 2–4% opacity maximum.

Do not use obvious fake paper textures.
